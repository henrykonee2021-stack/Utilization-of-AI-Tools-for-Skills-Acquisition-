Why This Matters

Opportunity shouldn't depend on income

Across Rivers State, many willing learners are held back not by ability but by circumstance — the cost of a training centre, a smartphone they don't own, or lessons that assume a reading level they haven't reached yet.

RiverSkills proposes to meet people where they are: free access at community hubs, an AI tutor that speaks plainly and patiently, and practice that adjusts to each learner instead of the other way around.

Who Can Join

Eligibility & how verification works

Priority is given to indigent and low-income households, verified through a simple, respectful process — not a bureaucratic one.

  • ✓ Resident of Rivers State, any of the 23 LGAs
  • ✓ From a household facing financial hardship
  • ✓ Age 15 and above — no upper age limit
  • ✓ Out-of-school youth, unemployed adults, and caregivers welcome
  • ✓ Priority slots for women, persons with disabilities, and displaced households

Verification, simplified

A short attestation from your Ward or Local Government Area confirms eligibility — no lengthy paperwork, no fees. Hub coordinators are trained to assist anyone unsure of the process.

If a hub reaches capacity, applicants are placed on a waiting list and contacted as soon as a seat opens.

What Success Could Look Like

An illustrative example, not a real testimony

A

Imagine Ada, a caregiver in Ahoada

Ada never finished secondary school and can't afford a fashion-design course in town. At her ward's learning hub, she works through a tailoring track on a shared tablet — the AI tutor explains each step aloud in plain English, and slows down or repeats a lesson whenever she needs it. In a few months, she has a certificate and enough skill to take in her first paying clients.
